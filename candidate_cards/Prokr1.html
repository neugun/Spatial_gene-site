Function in SAG: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
Prokr1 encodes prokineticin receptor 1 (GPR73), a class A GPCR for the secreted cysteine-rich proteins prokineticin-1 (EG-VEGF) and prokineticin-2 (Bv8). It couples to Gq and Gi, mobilising calcium and activating MAPK signalling. The prokineticin system controls gut motility, angiogenesis, nociception, neurogenesis and neuroinflammation, and its paralogue PROKR2 is essential for GnRH neuron migration and circadian output.
Region function
The nucleus sagulum is a small lateral tegmental cell group in the lateral midbrain next to the inferior colliculus and lateral lemniscus; it projects to the inferior colliculus and auditory thalamus and contributes to multisensory and auditory-limbic processing of salient or aversive sounds.
Published in this region
inferred No region-specific literature found. CNS prokineticin receptor expression has been mapped mainly to the suprachiasmatic nucleus, olfactory bulb, hypothalamus and spinal nociceptive circuits, with PROKR1 predominating in sensory and immune cells (Chen 2005; Lattanzi 2021); the sagulum itself is defined anatomically by its projection to the inferior colliculus (Henkel 1988) and neighbouring lateral tegmental circuits have recently been implicated in noise-induced negative valence (Zhou 2025).
Predicted function
Prediction: if PROKR1 is enriched in sagulum neurons, prokineticin-2 released from nearby tegmental or circadian sources would act through Gq to depolarise them and raise their gain, biasing inferior-colliculus and auditory-thalamic output toward salient or threatening sounds and providing a route by which time of day and inflammatory state modulate auditory salience. Local infusion of the PROKR antagonist PC1, or Prokr1 deletion, is predicted to reduce acoustic startle and defensive responses to loud sounds and to weaken noise-induced aversive learning, without changing hearing thresholds.
Innovation
★★★★★ 5/5 Neither the prokineticin system nor much else has been examined in the sagulum, yet it is an accessible node of the ascending auditory-aversive pathway with usable knockout and antagonist tools.
Tools
Prokr1 knockout mice; non-peptide PROKR antagonists PC1 and PC7; agonists Bv8/PROK2; anti-PROKR1 antibodies; the sagulum is reachable with retro-AAV or CAV2-Cre injected into the inferior colliculus; no Prokr1 driver line in common use.
