Function in PO: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
Adgrg5 encodes GPR114/ADGRG5, an adhesion G protein-coupled receptor. Its long extracellular region ends in a GAIN domain that self-cleaves, exposing a tethered peptide agonist (the Stachel sequence) which folds back into the seven-transmembrane core to activate Gs and raise cAMP. Cleavage is required for activation, and the receptor shows high constitutive and mechanically sensitive activity.
Region function
The posterior complex of the thalamus (PO) is a higher-order thalamic nucleus carrying whisker-touch, self-motion and nociceptive signals, relaying between brainstem, cortical layer 5 and secondary somatosensory/motor cortex rather than acting as a simple first-order relay.
Published in this region
none No region-specific literature found. Published ADGRG5 work is entirely biochemical and immunological - tethered-agonist activation and cleavage dependence (Bernadyn et al. 2023; Wilde et al. 2016), cryo-EM of Stachel-bound ADGRG receptors (Ping et al. 2022), and expression restricted to eosinophils, lymphocytes, monocytes and dendritic cells (Hamann et al. 2015). No study reports ADGRG5 in brain, thalamus or neurons.
Predicted function
Prediction: if the PO signal is genuinely neuronal rather than from perivascular or thalamic immune cells, ADGRG5 would most plausibly act as a constitutively active, mechanically tuned Gs/cAMP source in higher-order thalamocortical relay neurons, setting resting cAMP tone and thereby HCN-dependent burst-versus-tonic firing mode and the gain of layer-5 driver input. Knockout or Stachel blockade would then shift PO neurons toward burst firing and degrade whisker-touch and nociceptive transmission to secondary somatosensory cortex. The required first experiment is RNAscope plus a cell-type check to confirm neuronal expression.
Innovation
★★★★★ 5/5 The receptor has never been reported in brain at all, so any validated neuronal role in a higher-order thalamic nucleus would be entirely new.
Tools
No Cre/Flp driver, validated antibody or characterised brain knockout reported. Available chemistry only: dihydromunduletone (ADGRG1/ADGRG5 antagonist), gedunin- and khivorin-derived partial agonists, and synthetic Stachel peptide agonists. Detection currently requires RNAscope or a knock-in epitope tag; PO itself is accessible by standard thalamic AAV targeting.
