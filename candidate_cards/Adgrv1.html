Function in SCO: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
Adgrv1 encodes ADGRV1 (VLGR1/GPR98), the largest known GPCR: a roughly 6,300-residue adhesion receptor with a huge extracellular Calx-beta and pentraxin ectodomain, an autoproteolytic GAIN domain and a seven-transmembrane core. It is a structural member of the Usher syndrome type 2 protein network at hair-cell ankle links and photoreceptor connecting cilia. Mutations cause Usher syndrome 2C and audiogenic seizure susceptibility in mice.
Region function
The subcommissural organ is a tiny ependymal secretory gland at the entrance of the cerebral aqueduct beneath the posterior commissure; it releases SCO-spondin into the CSF, where it condenses into Reissner's fibre, and its failure causes aqueductal stenosis and hydrocephalus.
Published in this region
inferred No region-specific literature found. ADGRV1 has been characterised in cochlear hair-cell stereocilia, where it is required for proper stereocilia maturation (Yagi 2007), and at photoreceptor cilia; its ciliary and apical-membrane localisation is the closest link to the ciliated secretory ependymocytes of the subcommissural organ, but no SCO expression or function has been reported.
Predicted function
Prediction: because ADGRV1 organises ankle links and periciliary membrane complexes in other ciliated cells, in SCO ependymocytes it would most plausibly stabilise the apical and ciliary membrane and help couple cilia to CSF flow and to SCO-spondin secretion. Conditional deletion in ependyma is predicted to disorganise SCO cilia, reduce or misdirect Reissner's-fibre assembly, and mildly predispose to aqueductal obstruction and hydrocephalus, rather than to produce any behavioural phenotype; note also that SCO-mapped signal may partly reflect neighbouring posterior-commissure and periaqueductal tissue.
Innovation
★★★★☆ 4/5 A ciliary adhesion GPCR at a ciliated CSF-secreting gland is a plausible and untested link to hydrocephalus, and the knockout mice already exist.
Tools
