Function in OV: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
TPTE is a PTEN-family enzyme with a voltage-sensor-like transmembrane region, phosphatase domain and C2 tensin-type domain. Unlike PTEN it is membrane-anchored, acting on phosphoinositides to generate PI(4,5)P2 and restrain AKT/mTOR. Expression is essentially testis-restricted; loss causes defective meiotic double-strand break repair, sperm flagellar defects and low sperm count.
Region function
The OVLT is a barrier-free sensory circumventricular organ transducing plasma osmolality and sodium into thirst, vasopressin release and thermoregulatory adjustments.
Published in this region
inferred No region-specific literature found. Closest is TPTE being testis-specific, its only characterised in vivo role in spermatogenesis (Chen et al. 2026), whereas paralogue PTEN is broadly expressed in hypothalamic and lamina terminalis neurons, so any OVLT Tpte signal is unverified.
Predicted function
Prediction: if real, the signal would reflect TPTE acting as a local phosphoinositide phosphatase setting membrane PI(4,5)P2 in osmosensory cells. Because PI(4,5)P2 gates TRPV1/TRPV4 and mechanosensitive osmosensing channels, TPTE loss should shift the drinking and vasopressin threshold, not abolish thirst. AKT/mTOR control of metabolic load is an alternative; RNAscope with a testis positive control comes first, as weak vascular-tissue signal is often false positive.
Innovation
★★★☆☆ 3/5 Completely unstudied in brain, but the OVLT signal is unlikely to be genuine and no brain-usable reagents exist.
Tools
Tpte-deficient mouse (Chen et al. 2026); commercial anti-TPTE antibodies validated only for testis/tumour work; no Cre driver, pharmacology or AAV enhancer.
