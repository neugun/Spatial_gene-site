Function in AV: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
ADGRF4 (GPR115) is an adhesion GPCR: a seven-transmembrane receptor with a long extracellular region cleaved at a GAIN/GPS domain, so that a tethered Stachel peptide activates the receptor when the N-terminal fragment is shed under mechanical or ligand stress. Its known biology is epithelial: Chiba et al. 2020 showed it is required for enamel mineralisation via Car6, and Winkler et al. 2022 showed it drives keratinocyte stratification along KRT1 filaments.
Region function
The anteroventral thalamic nucleus relays mammillary body and subicular signals to retrosplenial and cingulate cortex within the extended hippocampal circuit, and carries a large head-direction cell population supporting spatial orientation.
Published in this region
none No region-specific literature found. The closest data are the IMPC Adgrf4 knockout (MGI:1925499), which reports only an eye/vision phenotype and no adult brain expression, and the absence of any Adgrf4 ISH experiment in the Allen Mouse Brain Atlas.
Predicted function
Prediction: as a mechanically gated adhesion GPCR, ADGRF4 in AV would most plausibly act at adhesive contacts between relay neurons and mammillothalamic or retrosplenial afferents, or during thalamocortical wiring, coupling contact force to Gq/G12-13 signalling. AV-restricted deletion should spare gross anatomy but degrade head-direction cell tuning and allocentric tasks. A Stachel agonist peptide plus AAV-Cre in floxed mice is a feasible first test.
Innovation
★★★★★ 5/5 An epithelial adhesion GPCR with zero CNS literature, paired with a head-direction circuit that has well-defined physiological readouts.
Tools
IMPC/KOMP Adgrf4 knockout and conditional-ready alleles (MGI:1925499); GPR115 antibodies validated in skin (Winkler 2022); generic adhesion-GPCR Stachel agonist peptides. No Cre driver or small-molecule ligand known.
