Function in SCH: evidence, prediction, innovation

Gene function
Grpr encodes the gastrin-releasing peptide receptor (BB2), a bombesin-family Gq-coupled GPCR driving PLCbeta, IP3/Ca2+ and ERK; its ligand GRP is the mammalian bombesin homolog. Known peripherally for mitogenic actions, it acts centrally in spinal dorsal horn GRPR interneurons relaying itch.
Region function
The suprachiasmatic nucleus is the master pacemaker; its retinorecipient core of VIP and GRP neurons passes photic input to the AVP-rich shell.
Published in this region
direct Direct. Aida et al. 2002: GRP conveys photic entrainment from ventral to dorsal SCN, inducing mPer1, mPer2 and c-Fos where GRPR is abundant. Karatsoreos et al. 2006: SCN GRPR binding varies with time of day under light-dark but not constant darkness (light- not clock-driven); GRPR-deficient mice show blunted photic phase shifting. Li et al. 2025 (Grp-iCre mice): chemogenetic stimulation of SCN GRP neurons phase-shifts behaviour, and they respond rapidly to light.
Predicted function
Prediction: GRPR on shell neurons is the receptor arm of an intra-SCN core-to-shell photic relay. SCN-restricted Grpr deletion should impair light-induced phase delays and shell Per1/Per2 induction, sparing free-running period; local RC-3095 at CT16 should block light-induced delays.
Innovation
★★☆☆☆ 2/5 The GRP ligand and SCN GRP neurons are well studied, but receptor-side, cell-type-specific Grpr manipulation remains undone.
Tools
Grp-iCre knock-in mice, Grpr-eGFP and Grpr-CreERT2 lines, Grpr knockout and floxed alleles; antagonists RC-3095 and PD176252, agonists GRP(18-27) and bombesin; partners via Vip-Cre, Vipr2 knockouts and Avp-Cre.
